Also confirmed: all six clients submit **inside** the 25 s window at delays 0/3/6/9/12/15, so

### Why n=6 makes the baselines valid

Both are arithmetic, verified in `src/sfl/server/baseline_aggregators.py`:

- **Krum**: `k = n − f − 2`. At n=3 that is **0**, clamped to `max(1, n−2) = 1`. At n=6 it is
  **3**, no clamp, and `n ≥ 2f+3` is satisfied.
- **Trimmed mean**: `k = floor(trim_ratio × n)`. At n=3 that is `floor(0.6) = 0` — it trimmed
  nothing and **was plain FedAvg** wearing a robust-aggregator label. At n=6 it is **1** per
  tail.

The driver refuses to start against a server reporting anything other than 6 registered
clients, so it cannot silently produce n=3 numbers into the n=6 checkpoint.

### Two traps handled

**`/admin/reset` MERGES overrides.** A value left unset persists from whichever run set it
last. `rerun_p08.py` documents this; the n=6 driver therefore states every gate and clip
parameter explicitly on each reset (`snas_threshold_flag`, `snas_threshold_quarantine`,
`fedavg_clip_ratio`, `snas_anomaly_basis`), so one aggregator's settings cannot carry into the
next.

**`results_dir` collisions.** Four adaptive-attack configs previously shared one directory and
silently overwrote each other (SESSION_2026-08-21 §5). All five n=6 configs were verified to
have distinct `results_dir` values before launching.

### One config was missing

`configs/experiment_baseline_median_e1_gradient_scaling_n6.yaml` did not exist — median was
added as a baseline during P1-3, after the n=6 configs were generated. Created by copying the
trimmed-mean n=6 config; verified to differ in **exactly one line** (`results_dir`).

## 4. Results — E1 gradient scaling, n=6, 5 seeds

| Aggregator | AUROC (n=6) | AUROC (n=3, 10 seeds) |
|---|---|---|
| trimmed mean | 0.9580 ± 0.0003 | 0.9569 ± 0.0005 |
| median | 0.9580 ± 0.0004 | (in Table 3, P1-3) |
| **SNAS** | **0.9569 ± 0.0002** | 0.9613 ± 0.0001 |
| Krum | 0.9566 ± 0.0002 | 0.9646 ± 0.0005 |
| FLTrust | 0.9550 ± 0.0002 | 0.9593 ± 0.0003 |

Trimmed mean and median agreeing to four decimals is expected, not suspicious: with 6 values,
trimming one per tail averages the middle 4, and the coordinate median is the midpoint of that
same band. On a symmetric honest distribution with one extreme outlier they nearly coincide. At
n=3 they diverged only because trimmed mean was degenerate.

## 5. The headline: Krum stops collapsing

Grid runs only — 50 selection events = 5 seeds × 10 rounds.

| Client | n=3 | n=6 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **300 / 300 (100 %)** | 1 (2.0 %) |
| 2 | — | 5 (10.0 %) |
| 3 | — | 18 (36.0 %) |
| 4 | — | 26 (52.0 %) |
| 5 | — | 0 |
| **1 (attacker)** | never | **0** |

At n=3, `k` clamped to 1 made honest scores tie bit-for-bit and `min()` broke the tie by
insertion order — Client 0, delay 0 s, every round. At n=6 Krum spreads across four honest
clients and **never selects the attacker**, which is Krum working as designed.

Note the direction: Krum **drops** from 0.9646 (n=3) to 0.9566 (n=6). Its apparent n=3
advantage over SNAS was the artifact inflating it, not robustness. That is the cleanest
possible evidence for the P0-2 disclosure.

### Re-deriving this number

The smoke test wrote into the **same** log, so a naive count returns **52**, not 50. The two
extra are both client 2 from the 2-round smoke test, separated from the grid by a 35.9-minute
gap. Filter by timestamp (grid started 00:20:53) or split at the largest gap:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -c @"
import json, collections
recs=[]
with open('results/label_private_splitfed/server_n6/server_metrics.jsonl') as f:
    for line in f:
        if 'krum_selected' not in line: continue
        r=json.loads(line)
        if r.get('aggregator')=='krum' and r.get('krum_selected') is True: recs.append(r)
recs.sort(key=lambda r: r['timestamp'])
gaps=[(recs[i+1]['timestamp']-recs[i]['timestamp'], i) for i in range(len(recs)-1)]
_, i = max(gaps)
grid = recs[i+1:]
print(len(grid), dict(collections.Counter(r['client_id'] for r in grid)))
"@
```

**Note the log path**: n=6 writes to `results/label_private_splitfed/server_n6/`, a different
directory from the n=3 log. `timestamp` is a float epoch, not an ISO string.

## 6. What this supports, and what it does not

**Defensible:** *at n=3 Krum's selection is decided by submission order; at n=6, where Krum is
validly configured, it discriminates genuinely and never selects the attacker — and SNAS
matches it (0.9569 vs 0.9566).* Trimmed mean is likewise a real measurement for the first time.

**Not supported:** "SNAS wins at n=6." All five aggregators cluster within 0.003, and the two
order statistics edge SNAS by ~0.001. Claiming a win here would be overreach a reviewer would
catch immediately.

The honest framing is that **E1 gradient scaling does not discriminate between defenses once
they are all validly configured**. SNAS's separation was always on E2 free-rider at n=3 (+0.033
over FLTrust, +0.085 over trimmed mean) — and **E2 is not in this grid**, because only E1
configs exist for n=6.

### Two gaps, if you want them closed

1. **No clean n=6 config exists**, so there is no n=6 baseline and no Δ column. The n=3 table
   uses clean 0.9651 as its reference. ~5 runs (~30 min) to add.
2. **E2 free-rider at n=6** would put SNAS's actual differentiator on validly-configured
   ground. Needs 4 new configs plus ~25 runs (~2.5 h).

Neither is required for the P0-2 claim, which rests entirely on §5. Both are affordable in five
days if you want a stronger supplementary table.
