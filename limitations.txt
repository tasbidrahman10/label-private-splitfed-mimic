Overall verdict

The paper is strongly aligned with the special issue, but I would not submit the present version without substantial revision. The topic directly matches the call’s emphasis on robustness, realistic failures, resource constraints, and healthcare applications. The special issue remains open until 28 August 2026. Official CFP

However, several methodological problems could lead to rejection. My provisional recommendation would be:

Major revision / likely reject in its current form

Strengths
The intersection of asynchronous SplitFed, staleness, and malicious clients is timely and relevant.
A low-client cross-silo setting is potentially realistic for healthcare.
The manuscript is commendably transparent about the alternating attack that defeats memoryless SNAS.
Ten-seed evaluation for the principal experiments is better than the common single-seed practice.
The contribution-entropy idea is useful, although its current comparison needs correction.
The paper is readable, professionally organized, and visually clean.

3. The robust-baseline comparison is not valid
Krum

Krum’s standard Byzantine-resilience condition is

n≥2f+3.

With three clients and one malicious client, at least five clients are required. Original Krum paper

If the implementation uses n=3,f=1, Krum has zero nearest neighbours in its score. Every score can therefore become zero, and deterministic tie-breaking may always select Client 0. Thus, the reported 880/880 Client-0 selections may be an implementation/configuration artifact—not evidence that Krum inherently “collapses federation.”

The number 880 is itself unexplained: two experiments, ten seeds, and ten rounds should not produce 880 ordinary Krum aggregation decisions.

Trimmed mean

A trim ratio of 0.2 deliberately trims zero coordinates at n=3. This is not a meaningful robust baseline. Coordinate-wise median should be evaluated, or the number of clients should be increased.

FLTrust

The reported FLTrust result is described as uniform averaging and is therefore not FLTrust. Genuine FLTrust requires a clean server root dataset and a server update used as the trust direction. Official FLTrust paper

If the server is permitted to hold root features and labels, it can instantiate the global encoder and compute an encoder gradient. If the threat model prohibits server root data, FLTrust should be marked inapplicable and omitted from the numerical table. A method that reduces to FedAvg should not be reported as an FLTrust result.

All baselines must be evaluated using the same seeds. Currently, trimmed mean, FLTrust, and synchronous SplitFed are partly single-seed results.

A malicious SplitFed client interacts with the server-side model throughout local training by sending activations and receiving gradients. It can poison the shared server head before encoder aggregation. Quarantining its submitted encoder does not undo malicious updates already applied to g

Therefore, “intrusion-resilient SplitFed” is broader than what has been demonstrated. The authors should either:

narrow the contribution explicitly to detecting aggregation-time encoder model-replacement attacks.
6. Experimental evidence is too limited for this journal

The study uses one task, one single-centre dataset, three constructed clients, a small MLP, and ten rounds. MIMIC-IV originates from Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, so ICU-unit partitions do not constitute independent hospitals. Official MIMIC-IV description

At minimum, add:

N≥5 so Krum can be correctly configured;
experiments with 5, 10, and preferably 20 clients;
different malicious-client fractions;
a second dataset, ideally multi-centre eICU;
per-client AUROC and AUPRC;
worst-client performance;
calibration or Brier score;
class prevalence and client-distribution statistics;
patient-level train/validation/test separation;
an untouched test set.

The manuscript currently reports validation AUROC without describing an independent test set.

7. The ablation does not isolate the anomaly gate

The “no-detection” condition disables both SNAS gating and norm clipping. Therefore, the reported gain cannot be attributed specifically to SNAS.

A factorial ablation is needed:

Configuration	Gate	Clipping	Staleness normalization
Async FedAvg	No	No	Yes
Clipping only	No	Yes	Yes
SNAS only	Yes	No	Yes
Full method	Yes	Yes	Yes
No-staleness SNAS	Yes	Yes	No

Cosine-only and norm-only versions should also be tested.

8. Detection claims require qualification

The gate is disabled during warm-up, yet the abstract reports 100% detection. If an attacker is active in round 1, overall submission-level detection cannot be 100%. Report:

post-warm-up detection rate;
overall detection rate including warm-up;
number of malicious and honest submissions;
detection delay;
confidence intervals for detection and false-positive rates.

“0% observed FPR” is appropriate; an unqualified “0% FPR” is too strong.

Additional issues
“Label-private” is misleading because the server evaluates the supervised loss, but the label location and transmission protocol are not clearly defined.
Table 4 compares SNAS in the clean condition with Krum under attack. Contribution entropy should be compared under identical scenarios and computed separately over honest clients.
Table 9 calls d(τ) an aggregation weight, although Equation (12) subsequently normalizes it. Its poisoning-power calculation ignores normalized shares and persistent model effects.
Threshold selection and sensitivity appear to use the same attack setting used for evaluation. A separate calibration procedure is required.
The preprocessing description is inadequate: the 266 features, cohort criteria, mortality horizon, imputation, normalization, sample counts, class balance, patient overlap, optimizer, learning rate, and activation functions are missing.
The quantum-FL and quantum-split-learning paragraphs are unrelated to the contribution and should be removed.
Reference 1 is bibliographically incorrect. FedAvg was published at AISTATS in 2017, not as a 2023 paper. Official PMLR record
FLTrust should be cited as the NDSS 2021 paper rather than only as a later arXiv version.
The title should be narrowed unless the threat model and experiments are substantially expanded.
Recommended decision

The manuscript has a good core research direction and excellent topical fit, but the asymmetric norm-score vulnerability, invalid baseline configuration, incomplete asynchronous protocol, and limited experimentation are submission blockers.

Given the 28 August deadline, I would submit only if the authors can, at minimum:

Correct Equation (9) and test inverse-scaling/zeroing attacks.
Redefine detection over version-relative updates.
Expand to at least five clients and rerun valid robust baselines.
Separate gating from clipping in the ablation.
Clarify the asynchronous event protocol and staleness handling.
Provide complete dataset, test-split, and training details.
Narrow all security, privacy, and clinical claims.

Without these corrections, the paper is likely to receive a rejection rather than a normal major-revision decision.
